Virtual quote

The outlined blocks left of the floor. They count in the price but nobody deposited them. They are why a coin can trade from its first second.

Quote in the pool

The money buyers have paid in. On the curve it is the filled area under the line; in the pool it is the solid blocks between the floor and the price.

Coins in the pool

The blocks right of the price. They thin out as the price climbs, and run on further than the picture shows. At the price, quote sets into coins and coins melt into quote: that front is the trade.

Lent out

Loans come out of the floor: the quote sitting right at the floor price, which is what the borrower's coins would fetch if sold last. Those coins are locked, and only selling them could reach that quote, so there is nothing to liquidate.

Drawn from the protocol's own formulas (see docs/bonding-curve.md): with liquidity L, virtual quote is L × √floor, quote in the pool is L × (√price − √floor), and pool coins are L ÷ √price. The bottom axis is the square root of the price, which is what makes every block worth the same. Fees are 2% a trade, with 60% healed on a buy or burned on a sell. On Uniswap v4 a heal or burn waits until it is worth one tick step; here it lands at once, as it does in v1.
