How Wavefront looks. It is drawn in the block language it shares with GlazeCorp: rounded blocks, a wave made of them, and a dark ground lit in afterglow colours: blue, violet, pink and amber.

The mark

The Tide: a coin made of blocks, filled to a wave line.

What it shows

The disc is a coin. The coloured blocks are what backs it, shading from blue at the bottom through violet to pink. The amber line on top is the wave: the edge where that backing is rising. The empty blocks above are room still to fill.

How it is built

A 9 by 9 grid, corners dropped to make a disc. Every block leaves a gap of 14% of its cell and rounds its corners by 22% of its own width, the same numbers as every other block in the system.

The wave line, the fill level and the order of the colours are fixed in the logo. Don't redraw them.

Three sizes

Each smaller size is redrawn on a coarser grid, so the blocks never get too fine to read.

Logo

The mark with the name in Funnel Display Medium. The gap is 28% of the mark's height and the type is 86% of it.

Wordmark

On its own only where the mark is already nearby.

Keep clear space of a quarter of the mark's height on every side. The smallest lockup is 20px tall; below that, use the mark alone.

Colour

A near-black ground, ash type and the Afterglow ramp: five colours, sea floor to foam. Tide, the violet, is the accent.

Ground and type

Meaning

Only for what they say: price up, price down, caution. Green is never a brand colour here.

On the dark ground Tide reads at :1 and Foam at :1, so both work for text. On white, Tide is only :1; use the darker violet there (:1). Text on a Tide button is the ground colour (:1). The ramp always runs in the same order, Trench up to Foam; don't shuffle it.

Blocks

Anything beyond the logo is built from the same block: patterns, dividers, meters. A shape made of blocks may come loose at its leading edge, and nowhere else.

The rules

Every Wavefront picture is drawn by the same few rules, so the same thing always looks the same. They come from the protocol: a floor that is locked in, and a market that moves above it.

RuleWhy
Blocks are the floorAnything at or below the floor price is solid: locked in, never taken back.
Fluid is the marketAnything above the floor price is liquid: a Foam line with bands of water under it, moving with every trade.
Up is priceIn every picture the floor sits at the bottom and the market above it.
Filled holds money, outlined is emptyA filled block is quote that is really there. An outline is lent out, not yet deposited, or the floor ahead of coins nobody has bought.
Fluid sets into blocks, never the reverseEvery trade sets a little of the market into the floor, and the floor only rises. A row that has just set glows in Foam.
Fluid never touches blocksThe water stops a little short of the floor, and is never drawn over a block.
Blocks for anything fixedThe logo, glyphs, icons and meters are blocks too: identities and counts.
The colours keep their orderFoam on top, then Surf, Tide, Deep and Trench, in fluid and in blocks alike.

Mechanics

One picture explains every mechanic: the price curve, price up the side against coins held along the bottom, drawn to scale from the protocol's formulas. Under the floor price it is blocks, the quote that guarantees the floor for the coins held. Above it, up to the curve, it is fluid. Each card shows the market after the action, with a dotted line where the curve was before.

Buy

The price moves up the curve. More floor fills in under the coins bought, and more market above it.

Sell

The price moves back down the curve. It can reach the floor and never go under it.

Heal

Quote goes in and nothing comes out. The whole curve lifts, and the floor with it.

Burn

Coins are destroyed. The supply ends sooner, and the floor rises under the coins that are left.

Borrow

A loan takes the floor under the coins borrowed against, and leaves the market above alone. The floor price stays put.

Don't

  • Recolour the mark or reorder its colours. It is the Afterglow ramp on the dark ground, or one flat colour.
  • Use the full mark below 48px. Switch to Small, then Micro.
  • Put the disc-less mark on a light ground, or on anything lighter than the surface colour.
  • Change the wave line, the fill level or the block spacing.
  • Stretch, rotate, outline or add shadows and glows.
  • Draw fluid below the floor or blocks above it, or let the two touch. Blocks are the floor; fluid is the market over it.
  • Set the name in another face, or type it as "WaveFront" or "Wave Front". It is one word: Wavefront.
